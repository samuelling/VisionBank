What is in the model

  • Optics and cones. sRGB → L, M, S excitations; eccentricity-dependent optical blur (extra for S); photocurrent low-pass; Weber gain control per cone class, y = (I − A)/(I + A + Idark), with A a slow local average. This gives von Kries adaptation and the stronger response to decrements.
  • Retinal ganglion cells. Midget (L- and M-centre, mixed surround, so L − M opponency falls out of the wiring and fades with eccentricity), parasol (transient, divisive contrast gain control, rectified bipolar subunits), small bistratified (S − (L+M), no S signal in the foveola). Receptive fields grow with eccentricity; ON and OFF cells are rectified separately.
  • LGN. Parvo, magno and konio relays with a spike threshold, a divisive suppressive field wider than the receptive field, and a relay gain you can treat as arousal state.
  • V1. Quadrature Gabor pairs at 8 orientations × 4 octave-spaced SF bands; half-squaring and the energy model for complex cells; divisive normalization with a local cross-orientation, cross-SF pool and an orientation-tuned surround; slow orientation-specific contrast adaptation that shifts c50; direction from phase advance; single- and double-opponent colour. Preferred SF falls with eccentricity as 1/(0.12·E + 0.35) cycles/deg.
  • Flat map. V1 activity laid out on cortex with M = 17.3/(E + 0.75) mm/deg.

Limits. The camera sets the ceiling: at 30 frames/s the magno pathway is temporally undersampled, and foveal midget cells are finer than the pixels unless you shrink the field of view. One eye only, so no disparity. No noise, no spikes, no corticogeniculate feedback, no rods. Parameter values follow macaque physiology and human fMRI where available and are all editable.
